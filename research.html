Definition

For this semester-long discovery project, we must choose a feeling. My chosen feeling is “curious”. Curious means wanting to learn, making you question and think, and wanting to explore something. Curiosity can be both negative and positive depending on context. Sometimes, things can be strange and cause you to be curious, or something sparks your interest, and you become curious.

Color Association

Chartreuse: It communicates boldness, youth, vitality, and creativity. Butter: happiness without danger, sunshine, optimism, and happiness in most cultures. Orange: energy, youth, and happiness; the creative center/second chakra in Eastern philosophy. Primary colors together invoke curiosity.

History

In ancient and Biblical contexts, a wide-ranging curiosity was often viewed as a distraction or an imbalance of the soul. In early Christian tradition association it is associated with spiritual straying, such as Adam and Eve (Adama and Eve were curious in the Garden of Eden and were punished for wanting to know more than they should. Christians viewed curiosity as sinful until the Renaissance. The pursuit of knowledge was celebrated in the Greek and Roman eras. Curiosity now is a part of our everyday lives in the United States. Societies that believe in progress, creativity, and innovation recognize that creativity in the minds of the people is extremely valuable. Stemming from education, work, and personal endeavors. Many corporate companies show curiosity in their key factors to their work, such as Microsoft and Google. In contrast, more narrow-minded societies that value order do not allow curiosity.

In photography

Having curiosity within photography allows the viewer to fill in the gaps and engage longer with the photo. Behavioral economist George Loewenstein calls this “the gap of knowledge”. Meaning, curiosity comes from when we perceive a gap in things we know. Like a good mystery movie or book. We stay engaged because we want to find more information. However, if the gap is too large, the viewer becomes uninterested. An effective image can involve the viewer to think “what is happening here”. A good photo has contrast, tension, and the right amount of curiosity.
